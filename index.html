01 — Sequence

16 tracks. Each with its own time.

1 track per MIDI channel, up to 64 steps each. Slow a track to 1/64 and a single step lasts 4 bars — that is 256 bars of sequence for phrases that really develop. Run tracks at different lengths and speeds for polymeters and polyrhythms that never repeat the same way twice. Fill a track any way you like: TR-REC step entry, live recording, Björklund (Euclidean) rhythms, or the generative engines.

  • DRUM, MONO and POLY track types
  • Speeds down to 1/64 — a step can last 4 bars, up to 256 bars per track
  • TR-REC step entry, live recording, Björklund / Euclidean generation
  • Overdub recording with 4 layers
  • Copy, paste, halve or double the length, bar loop
  • Pattern edit lock — hold SEQ while you turn a parameter and the pattern-edit overlay locks
  • Gate, velocity, accent, glide, tie and probability per step
  • Every generated sequence stays editable — tweak, nudge or rewrite it step by step
  • Internal clock or external MIDI sync

02 — Learn

The theory, lit up.

The pads teach as well as play. In KEY mode the notes of the current scale light up across the keyboard, so the shape of a mode is something you see before you hear it. Chords are named by degree in Roman numerals — I, ii, ♭VII — and tension tones, harmonic movement, inversions and tessitura stop being abstractions: they are pads glowing under your fingers while the progression runs.

  • 54 scales — chromatic, the church modes, harmonic and melodic minor, harmonic major, pentatonic and blues, symmetric, bebop, Messiaen modes, maqam and gamelan
  • Chord movement, guide tones and inversions visible in real time
  • Tessitura and voicing spread map the range you are working in

03 — Progress

A progression engine with taste.

In KEY 4 the instrument proposes chords from a real genre table: 18 transition matrices, chord qualities, inversions and rhythm templates measured from the music itself. A Viterbi pass then finds the most musical path through the matrix, weighing where you are against where you came from, so the proposal follows the grammar of the style instead of hopping from chord to chord at random. Tap to accept, hold to bake it into the sequence.

  • 18 genre grammars — POP · ROCK · JAZZ · CLASSICAL · FOLK · COUNTRY · BLUES · RNB · FUNK · METAL · HIPHOP · ELECTRONIC · REGGAE · DUBTECHNO · GOSPEL · LATIN · CINEMATIC · MODAL
  • Viterbi decoding scores every path through the genre and proposes the best one
  • Cadence bands: authentic · plagal · deceptive · half
  • Phrase templates FREE / AABB / ABAB / ABAC / AABA
  • Scale, root, octave and chord latch on the encoders

04 — Follow

Chain tracks into harmony.

Point 1 track at another and it listens. A lead follows your chords, a harmony track reads your bassline, a bass locks to the progression. Change 1 part and the others move with it.

  • Bass → Harmonizer → Chords → Lead chains
  • Follow, or freeze the chord of the moment
  • Chord-tone and guide-tone mapping

05 — Arpeggiate

9 ways to walk a chord.

Set any MONO track to ARP and it becomes an arpeggiator. Span the chord over up to 4 octaves, pick a walk order, then arm a sub-page and the whole encoder row turns into that page's controls.

  • Walks: up, down, up-down, random ×2, out-in, in-out, converge, diverge
  • 4 sub-pages — ARP ORDER · ARP MOTION · ARP GENERATIVE · ARP OPTIONS
  • Markov and random-walk engines with seed, rate, depth and memory

    06 — Generate

    Let it write the bassline.

    • Strum spread; HAND mode turns the keys into strings
    • Classic random or Euclidean rhythms
    • 18 drum styles with live fill and motion

    07 — Control

    8 knobs. Your whole studio.

    Switch to encoder mode and the 8 knobs become MIDI CC controllers, with banks for 36 CCs. Your DAW sends values back, so the knobs always match. Drive Ableton Live clips and devices too.

    • CC banks F1–F4
    • 2-way value feedback
    • Ableton Live clip and device control

    3 families · 12 modes

    1 pad picks the mode.
    The screen tells you where you are.

    Each family has 4 variants. The instrument remembers the last one you used.

    Open by design

    Yours to open up.

    The firmware is open source and the hardware is open hardware. Nothing here is sealed: read the code, change how a mode behaves, add a generator of your own, or wire the panel into the rest of your rig.

    FW

    Open firmware

    Source you can read, rebuild and flash back over USB

    2. AI friendly — small, legible, clearly named
    3. Add a generator, change a mode, keep the panel
    4. Re-flash over USB whenever you like
